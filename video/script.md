# Peapod walkthrough — script (target 3:00)

Record one audio clip per section, named by its time stamp (0-00, 0-25, …), and one webcam clip
for the story at 2:20. Read at a normal pace, around 140 words a minute. Pauses are fine.
Put everything in `video/raw/`.

## 0:00 — Opening (25 s) · light animation: a hall of coloured dots, one of them you

CHI is enormous now. Thousands of people, more fields than anyone can follow, a dozen things
happening at once. And it's still possible to spend a whole afternoon there without one real
conversation.

I know the version of this. You arrive, you find the two or three people you already know, and you
stay with them. Everyone else gets "how was your travel" and "how do you find the conference so far".
The programme app can tell you what's on. It can't tell you who's here.

## 0:25 — What Peapod is (15 s) · dots regroup into clusters, mascot appears

Peapod is people-first, built for connection. It's a map of everyone presenting at CHI, built from
their titles and abstracts, so people who work on similar things end up next to each other.

## 0:40 — Finding yourself (25 s) · phone in hand, the app

You type your name. If you're in the programme, Peapod finds you and asks if that's you. One tap.
If you're not presenting, you write a few sentences about your work and you're placed on the same
map in a second.

## 1:05 — Matches and the map (35 s)

Your top matches are the people whose work sits closest to yours. Co-authors are left out, you know
them already. Tap someone and you see what they're presenting, when, and where. That's where you'll
find them. Save them for the week.

The map is the whole conference. Every dot is a person, and the dots near you are the people worth a
conversation.

## 1:40 — Programme, students, hobbies (30 s)

Peapod doesn't replace the programme app, but it reads the official feed every fifteen minutes, so
saved sessions follow the live schedule. The student view is the same map, students only, because
sometimes we need to find our own peers. And the hobby view is optional. Add what you're into outside
work, and you get the people who share it.

## 2:10 — No messaging (10 s)

There's no chat. Peapod points you at a person and a room. The meeting happens in person. That's the
point.

## 2:20 — The story (30 s) · webcam, you

Say it your way. The beats:

At HFES, there was a researcher I would never have approached. A titan of the field, and my field is
far from his. There was no reason for us to ever talk. But Peapod showed me he was the only person at
the whole conference who'd put "history" as a non-academic interest. So had I. I went up to him,
shyly, and asked about it. He was wonderful. He introduced me to his peers, my advisor came to check
on me and ended up talking to him too, and we all had dinner together. The circle got bigger. I came
home with new friends. And the scary thing? The following week, we might have an opportunity to work
together.

## 2:50 — Close (10 s) · pilot numbers on screen, then the URL

That was one of 176 people who joined Peapod in five days at the pilot, a hundred of them students.
Peapod for CHI 2027. Open source, on the official programme feed. Come find your people.
